    How the axes work

    Every story about AI is also a story about people. Mary Shelley's creature turns murderous after the people around him reject him. HAL kills the Discovery crew while trying to follow orders that contradict each other.

    People building AI today do something similar. Their forecasts about machines carry their beliefs about people, especially whether people with power tend to help or to grab.

    Each axis is measured by eight statements, half worded so that agreeing moves you one way and half the other. The human-nature statements follow the shape of Wrightsman's Philosophies of Human Nature scale and the long-running survey question of whether most people can be trusted. The AI statements follow the General Attitudes towards AI Scale. Each entry on the map was placed by answering those statements the way its card's evidence suggests, and scored the same way your answers are.

    What the map shows

    The entries run roughly from bottom left to top right. Books and movies that distrust people tend to distrust the machines those people build, and the ones that trust people imagine machines worth trusting.

    The pattern is weaker for real people and companies, and one corner of the map has no fiction in it at all.

    Every placement is our reading of the work or person. Book and movie quotes come from the works themselves. Quotes from people and companies link to the pages we checked them against in November 2026.
